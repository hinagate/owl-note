title: "Deriving the quadratic formula (for tutoring)"
lang: en

For Sunday's tutoring session — showing *where* the formula comes from instead of
just handing it over. Start from the general quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.

Divide through by $a$ and move the constant across:

$$x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x = -\frac{c}{a}$$

Complete the square — add $\left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2$ to both sides:

$$\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}$$

Take the square root of both sides (that's where the $\pm$ enters), and solve:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The bit under the root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is the **discriminant** — positive means two
real roots, zero means one, negative means the roots are complex. That's the part
they should actually remember. The full formula they can always re-derive if they
can complete the square.
